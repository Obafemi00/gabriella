## Notes for the reviewer

- **circumstance**: Usually used in the plural (“circumstances”). Consider changing the word to “circumstances”; the singular sentence is correct but less natural.
- **comprise**: “be comprised of” is left out on purpose: many teachers and examiners treat it as an error. Use “X comprises Y” or “X is made up of Y”.
- **notwithstanding**: Very formal; fine in Writing, rare in Speaking.
- **respectively**: Has no strong collocations; the entries show its usual Task 1 pattern.
- **likewise**: Has few fixed collocations; mostly used as a linker at the start of a clause.
- **conversely**: Has few fixed collocations; mostly used as a linker at the start of a clause.
- **hence**: “hence” is formal; in Speaking, “so” or “that's why” is more natural.
- **decline**: Also a verb (“numbers decline”); written as the noun.
- **challenge**: Also a verb (“challenge an idea”); written as the noun.
- **advocate**: Also a noun (“a strong advocate of”); written as the verb.
