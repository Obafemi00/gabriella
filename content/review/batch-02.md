## Notes for the reviewer

- **via**: “via” is a preposition. There is no preposition type, so it is listed as “phrase”.
- **uniform**: Written as the adjective (academic use). The noun (“school uniform”) is more common but basic; it is a common IELTS Speaking topic if you would rather use that sense.
- **orient**: British English often uses “orientate”; both are correct. “oriented towards” is the most useful form.
- **parameter**: Usually plural (“parameters”); the singular sentence is correct for the gap-fill.
- **interval**: Usually seen as “at regular intervals”; the example uses the singular for the gap-fill.
- **grant**: Also a verb (“grant permission”); written as the noun.
- **shift**: Also a verb and a work period (“night shift”); written as the noun (change).
- **substitute**: Also a verb (“substitute X for Y”); written as the noun.
- **supplement**: Also a verb (“supplement your income”); written as the noun.
- **discipline**: Two senses (rules; a subject of study). The example uses the rules sense; the collocation “academic discipline” shows the other.
